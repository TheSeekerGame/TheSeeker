# Open Brand

The Seeker is an open-source brand. The game in this repository, headed for a Steam release, is one branch of it, the trunk of a tree meant to grow in every direction: other games, art projects, 3D worlds, music, prints, and merch. Anyone can build on it, with or without experience, and every creation adds to the same culture and lore.

The brand belongs to no company and to no single person. Its code, art, audio, and lore are open to everyone. What people hold instead is cultural ownership: collectibles that have recorded the project on-chain since its first days.

| Art, animation, and audio | `assets/` | CC BY 4.0 |

You can copy, remix, fork, and sell anything built from these, including commercially. CC BY asks for one thing in return: credit The Seeker as the source. [LICENSE.md](https://github.com/TheSeekerGame/TheSeeker/blob/main/LICENSE.md) has a ready-made attribution line.

Your version doesn't have to be a 2D pixel-art metroidvania. Hand the source, assets, and lore to an AI agent and ask for a 3D world, a card game, an animation, or a sculpture to print. The repository is organized for agents: [AGENTS.md](https://github.com/TheSeekerGame/TheSeeker/blob/main/AGENTS.md) maps the code. A `SKILL.md` that teaches any agent the vision, art style, and atmosphere in one step is on the way, followed by the code that connects the game to collectibles.

## Why open source

AI turns any product into something that can be rebuilt from a screenshot and a prompt. Copyright still exists, but when anyone can recreate a game in an afternoon, it can't be the thing a creative project depends on. The Seeker has been open source from the start, on the assumption that this moment would come.

Instead of fighting copies, the brand invites them. Open source is viral: every fork, remix, and derivative puts The Seeker's world in front of a new audience, and any of them can feed ideas back into it.

## Ownership through collectibles

Open source creates attention and culture but not, on its own, a way for the people who build a brand to share in what it becomes. Collectibles do that. The first trading cards of a franchise matter more the more its world grows; the brand's collectibles play the same role for every creation built on it.

- **Provenance.** The Seeker's development has been inscribed on Bitcoin as Ordinals since 2023, including some of the first ten thousand inscriptions ever made. That timestamped record predates the current wave of AI tools, and every version of The Seeker, whoever builds it, traces back to it.
- **Value comes from culture, not from gameplay.** The game is open source and playable without owning anything, and nothing about it is pay-to-win. Collectibles capture the attention the brand earns instead of charging players for it.
- **Holders as game masters.** Holders take part in shaping the world. Planned features let them change the game world in real time and place and modify characters in it. Holders also share a private chat with the team for suggestions, early releases, and giveaways.
- **Design stays independent.** Monetizing attention rather than the game keeps profit out of design decisions, keeps middlemen out of the way, and lets anyone contribute as much or as little as they like.

Collectibles are art and community membership. Nothing here promises financial returns.

## Collections

### The Seeker (Ordinals)

[The Seeker](https://ord.net/collection/the-seeker) documents the game's development as it happens. Many of its first inscriptions belong to the Sub-10K club, the first ten thousand inscriptions on Bitcoin.

### Planetary Primes (Ordinals)

[Planetary Primes](https://ord.net/collection/planetaryprimes) are strange mathematical artifacts inscribed on prime-numbered sats, in keeping with the mathematical layer of the [lore](lore.md). They give community access and giveaways, and holders are planned to be able to make real-time changes to the game world once the game releases.

### Planetary Guardians (Ordinals)

[Planetary Guardians](https://ord.net/collection/planetary_guardians) are alien demigods that will appear on the planet. Holders are planned to be able to modify their guardians in the game and choose where they stand. They also work as profile pictures.

### SEEKER NET (Robinhood Chain)

[SEEKER NET](https://opensea.io/collection/seeker-net) extends the world to a new chain. It is a complete game and a social, economic, and art experiment in its own right: 2,222 seekers charge toward activation, and the path each one takes becomes its final art.

- Every seeker below 9,000 charge gains 50 charge a day. At 9,000 it activates.
- Moving a seeker (any sale, gift, or transfer) releases a quarter of its charge, shared equally among all the other seekers still charging. Every move makes everyone else stronger.
- **Discharge** records a seeker's latest release, and **Uplink** records everything it has ever given to the network.
- Two quests: the main quest is charge, holding until activation; the side quest is Discharge and Uplink, powering the network on the way there. There is no single best strategy, only different paths, and the rarest seekers will be the ones that took the paths fewer people chose.
- No new NFTs are added. Instead, top pixel artists keep adding traits to the existing seekers, until every decision, including community participation, shows up in the art.

The collection is fully animated.

## Build on it

1. Clone the [repository](https://github.com/TheSeekerGame/TheSeeker), or point your AI agent at it.
2. Read the [design vision](README.md), [lore](lore.md), and [art direction](art-direction.md) to get the feel of the world.
3. Make something, in any medium, credit The Seeker, and share it.

Every interesting creation grows the brand, and the brand belongs to everyone who builds it.
